Setting Slate up

  • Open the downloaded file and drag Slate into your Applications folder. If you open Slate straight from the download instead, it offers to move itself there.
  • Open Slate from Applications. It has no window. Look for the waveform in the menu bar at the top of the screen.
  • Slate asks for the microphone, then for accessibility. It needs both. There is a short setup guide on the first run that walks you through them.
  • The speech model downloads once, about 600 MB. Settings › General shows the progress. If the download stops partway, because you quit, the Mac slept, or the Wi‑Fi dropped, Slate carries on from where it stopped the next time it opens.
  • Click into any text box, hold Right Option, and talk. Let go when you are done.

You can bring the setup guide back at any time from the menu bar: Show setup guide.

The talk key does nothing

  • Open System Settings › Privacy & Security › Accessibility and check that Slate is switched on. If it already was, macOS may be holding a stale entry from before an update. Slate notices this and offers Repair in the menu bar and in Settings: it removes its own entry and asks macOS again.
  • Check the menu bar. If the waveform is not there, Slate is not running.
  • Try a different key in Settings › General › Talk key. Some keyboard tools take Right Option for themselves.
  • If you prefer not to hold anything, set When pressed to tap once to start and tap again to stop.
  • If your 14 days are up, dictation stops until a key is entered. Settings › License shows where you stand. See The trial ended.

Slate can’t hear me

If the waveform in the menu bar has a line through it, Slate is missing a permission. Click it and the menu says which one.

  • If it says the microphone is off, choose Turn On Microphone… in that menu. Or open System Settings › Privacy & Security › Microphone and switch Slate on.
  • If it says the microphone is blocked by this Mac’s settings, it cannot be switched on from Slate. That usually means a work or school manages the Mac, and only they can allow it.
  • Settings › General › Permissions shows both permissions and whether each one is on.

The menu bar icon is missing

On a MacBook with a notch, a crowded menu bar can push the icon under the cutout, where you cannot see or click it. Quit Slate, run this in Terminal, then open Slate again:

defaults write com.slate.dictation "NSStatusItem Preferred Position Item-0" -float 250

That pins the icon to the right of the notch. Hiding a few other menu bar items helps too.

The text goes to the wrong place

Slate puts the text into the app you were in when you started talking, so switching apps mid-sentence does not send your words somewhere unexpected.

Some apps do not tell macOS where the cursor is. In those, Slate goes by the gap since you last spoke: a short gap carries the sentence on, a long one starts fresh. Click where you want the words before you talk and it is right every time.

A word keeps coming out wrong

Open Settings › Dictionary and add it: what you say on one side, what should be written on the other. Names, jargon, and shorthand all work. Your entries win over everything else, including the built-in spellings.

Dictating a list

Say the number, pause, then the item: “one, milk, two, eggs, three, bread”. If the pauses are not landing, say the word “comma” between the parts instead. That works every time. Counting, prices, and clock times stay as plain sentences.

Turning the cleanup off

Settings › General › Cleanup before inserting. Each rule has its own switch: filler sounds, stutters, and spoken commands.

Speaking another language

Settings › General › Speech model. English is the fastest and most accurate. The multilingual model covers 25 European languages and works out which one you are speaking. It downloads once, about 460 MB. The cleanup rules are written for English, so most stand down in that mode.

What is kept, and where

Everything Slate keeps is on your Mac:

  • ~/Library/Application Support/Slate/ holds your history (text only), your notes, your dictionary, your license record, and a plain text log for support.
  • ~/Library/Preferences/com.slate.dictation.plist holds your settings and your totals, and a copy of your license key if you have one.
  • ~/Library/Application Support/FluidAudio/Models/ holds the speech models.

Settings › History lets you keep the history, keep only the last 24 hours, or switch it off. Switching it off, or pressing Clear All, deletes the history file from your Mac straight away. Audio is never written to disk. More detail is on the privacy page.

What you need

An Apple silicon Mac, M1 or later, and macOS 14.4 or later. Speech runs on the Neural Engine, which Intel Macs do not have. There is no Windows, iPhone, or web version.

Updates

Slate can update itself. Settings › About holds the switch and a Check now button, and the menu bar has Check for updates as well.

It is off until you say yes. Slate asks once, the first time you open it after setup, and keeps whatever you answer. A check asks this site for one small file listing the newest version, and nothing about you or your Mac goes with it. A new version is installed only if it is signed with Slate's key.

You can also just download the new version from this site and drop it into Applications yourself. Either way your settings, dictionary, and history stay where they are.

A license covers Slate 2 and every Slate 2 update. If there is ever a Slate 3, it may be a paid upgrade, and the Slate 2 you have keeps working either way.

Moving to a new Mac

A license is for one Mac at a time. To move it:

  • On the old Mac, open Settings › License and press Deactivate This Mac…, which frees your key.
  • On the new Mac, install Slate, open Settings › License, paste the same key, and press Activate.

If the old Mac is lost, broken, or already wiped, email contact@bluetomatostudio.com from the address you bought with. The old Mac is taken off your key, and then you can activate on the new one. The same goes if Activate says the key is already active on another Mac.

Uninstalling

Moving to another Mac, or selling or giving this one away? First open Settings › License and press Deactivate This Mac…, so your key is free for the next Mac.

  • Quit Slate from the menu bar.
  • Drag Slate from Applications to the Trash.
  • Delete ~/Library/Application Support/Slate. That holds your history, notes, dictionary, log, and license record.
  • Open Terminal and run defaults delete com.slate.dictation to remove Slate’s settings.
  • Delete ~/Library/Application Support/FluidAudio to remove the speech models and get that space back. That is about 1 GB if you used both.
  • Remove Slate from System Settings › Privacy & Security, under both Accessibility and Microphone.

The Slate folder and the settings both hold your license key, so after these steps the key is gone from this Mac. To use Slate here again, paste the key back into Settings › License. If you did not deactivate first, Activate will say the key is already active on another Mac. Email and the seat is freed.

If you had Slate before it went on sale, that record goes too. Leave the folder and the settings in place if you might come back.
